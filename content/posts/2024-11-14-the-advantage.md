**TL;DR:** In _The Advantage_, Patrick Lencioni argues that organisational
health can be a durable competitive advantage. His framework emphasises trust,
constructive conflict, commitment, accountability and clear communication. It is
a leadership model to test in context, not evidence that culture outweighs
strategy or every business metric.

points as a note to self.

## Central Thesis

Lencioni's central claim is that organisational health is a major competitive
advantage that is often overlooked in favour of "smart" business decisions. The
strength of that wording belongs to the author; this post summarises the
framework rather than validating it empirically.

## Key components of organisational health

### Leadership team structure

- Optimal size: 3-10 people
- Built on trust and vulnerability
- Values collective success over individual achievement

### Core principles

- Trust and vulnerability as foundations
- Accountability at all levels
- Commitment to collective goals
- Clear communication and expectations

### Operational excellence

- Regular, focused meetings with specific purposes
- Clear distinction between strategic and tactical discussions
- Emphasis on debate and healthy conflict resolution
- Continuous progress monitoring

### People and culture

- Hire for clearly defined behaviours and values, while avoiding "cultural fit"
  as a proxy for similarity
- Focus on values alignment in recruitment
- Reward behaviour that aligns with organisational values
- Foster an environment of open communication

### Critical success factors

- Minimal internal politics
- High clarity in communication
- Clear decision-making processes
- Low employee turnover
- High morale and productivity

## Conclusion

The book's useful message is that strategy is difficult to execute when
leadership is distrustful, priorities are unclear or disagreement stays hidden.
Its checklists can prompt a team to examine those conditions. Organisational
health should complement evidence about customers, finances and operations, not
replace it.
