**TL;DR:** Jeremy Howard suggests studying earlier technology cycles instead of
pretending to predict AI's path. A practical response is to combine domain
knowledge with hands-on use of AI, learn through small projects and look for
neglected problems. This is a preparation strategy, not a promise of success.

<!--more-->

## Introduction

[Jeremy Howard](https://jeremy.fast.ai/) co-created
[ULMFiT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.06146), an influential method for transfer
learning in NLP, and co-founded [fast.ai](https://course.fast.ai/) and
[Answer.AI](https://www.answer.ai/). When
[asked about preparing for AI](https://x.com/chrisbarber/status/1888037803566747942),
he focused on practical lessons from earlier technology cycles rather than a
precise forecast.

## Why This Matters Now

Comparisons between AI and the early internet are imperfect, but they prompt
useful questions. Which capabilities are improving quickly? Which constraints
remain stubborn? What becomes possible when cost or access crosses a threshold?
History cannot supply a forecast, but it can make assumptions easier to see.

## Key Insights on Technology Evolution

Howard emphasises a crucial pattern: technology doesn't just grow linearly. Each
innovation follows a "hockey stick" growth curve before flattening into a
sigmoid.

More importantly, new "hockey sticks" emerge unexpectedly in different areas.
This pattern repeats "like clockwork" making historical understanding more
valuable than future predictions.

## Practical Preparation Strategy

Rather than trying to predict AI's future, Howard advocates for:

- Embracing uncertainty while avoiding both dismissive fear and blind hype
- Taking a counter-cyclical approach: pursuing opportunities others overlook
- Investing time in learning AI tools, accepting poor early results as part of
  the process
- Combining AI capabilities with deep domain expertise
- Building practical knowledge through side projects and community engagement

## The Education Perspective

Howard challenges traditional educational paths, suggesting alternatives:

- Self-directed learning through resources such as
  [fast.ai](https://course.fast.ai/)
- Multiple side hustles to build practical experience
- Community building with like-minded innovators
- Using AI itself to learn technical skills
- Developing both technical and human skills as a generalist

## Conclusion

The key takeaway is not a prediction. It is a way to act under uncertainty:
learn enough history to recognise recurring patterns, build enough to test
present capabilities and combine the technology with knowledge of a real domain.

Experts cannot predict AI's path reliably either. Small experiments, explicit
assumptions and a willingness to revise them provide a more grounded preparation
than either hype or dismissal.
